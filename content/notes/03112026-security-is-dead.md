The age we are entering, where an AI agent would do the work that a security person is doing, is disappointing but, at the same time, exciting for what the future holds.

I learnt security when I was in college and got excited about hacking during a hacking workshop. The initial days of excitement, using Linux, tinkering with Kali Linux tools, playing CTFs, and going the way I wanted to for serendipity, with no destination in mind, made me love the work I do and learn more.

But it is disappointing to see that AI will do that work. Even though I can do the same work, if the same work can be done better by AI agents, then the question is: why should one put effort into doing the wrong things when AI knows better than me and other humans, only to end up doing less futile work?

It is like asking why I should take on the hard part of manual security testing. The motivation to learn security by hand and read security blogs written by humans, rather than having AI find things for me, is fading. There is not much left for me to learn from the process because I can just ask AI to do the work and review it. This drives me to feel less excited about the process.

What is not dead is the job. In the short to medium term, jobs are going to be there. In the longer term, "I don't know" will be the right answer. The landscape has changed, and new threats will emerge. New compliance requirements will emerge, too.

But the excitement is tanking. Now I have to figure out how to get that excitement back, change my frame of reference, and maybe try a new domain that excites me.

I am hoping for a positive future for all of us. We have to definitively postmortem the old security process of discovering bugs, give some respect to it, and make peace with the new process.
